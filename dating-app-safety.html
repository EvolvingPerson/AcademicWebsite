Dating App Safety

8 Steps for Vetting a Dating App Profile

Eight questions to ask before you trust a match. I teach these in Health and the Individual (HE 111) at York College, and they work best when you use them before you meet, not after.

  • Photos
  • Bio
  • Engagement
  • Online presence
  • Money and personal information
  • Pace
  • Consistency
  • Verification and intuition
The eight steps

Check the profile, then check the pattern

The first four steps are about the profile itself. The last four are about how the person behaves once you start talking. No single answer proves a profile is fake, but several warning signs together are worth taking seriously.

1

Profile photos

  • Are the photos too good to be true, like professional model shots?
  • Does a reverse image search turn the photo up on stock photo sites, or show it is widely used elsewhere?
2

Profile details and bio

  • Is the bio detailed, realistic, and consistent, or vague and generic?
  • Are there spelling and grammar errors that seem out of place for the background the person claims?
3

Activity and engagement

  • Do their replies sound human and fit the conversation, or do they feel automated or off topic?
  • Are they overly eager or pushy about moving the conversation to another app or platform very quickly?
4

Social media and online presence

  • Can you find a linked social media account, and does it look real, with a history of posts and interactions?
  • Is their story consistent across platforms, or do the details not match?
5

Asking for money or personal information

  • Do they quickly bring up financial hardship or ask for money? That is a red flag for a scam profile.
  • Are they trying to collect personal information from you early, before any real trust or connection exists?
6

Too fast, too soon

  • Are they professing love or a deep connection unrealistically quickly, before you have met in person?
  • Do they dodge questions about meeting, or always have an excuse not to video chat or meet?
7

Consistency in communication

  • Is the way they communicate consistent, or does it not match their supposed lifestyle or time zone?
  • Do they disappear and reappear without explanation?
8

Other things to consider

  • Use the app's verification features, and favor profiles that are verified.
  • Trust your intuition. If something feels off, take that seriously.
  • Cross check what they tell you against what you can confirm on your own.
Why it matters

The quiet harms are the ones that hurt most

In my survey of 867 U.S. adults who had used a dating app in the past 60 days, 89 percent had experienced at least one harm in that window. The most common harms, like unwanted sexual messages, were the least distressing. The rarer ones, like pressure after saying no and someone finding you offline, were the most distressing, and the in-app block button reaches them least.

Vetting a profile is a first step. If something does happen, tell someone you trust, save the messages, and make a plan.
